The question

How do specialized flamenco periodicals — Candil, Jaleo, and their peers — construct flamenco legitimacy across Spanish and Anglophone contexts?

A
Through which modes is legitimacy constructed? We posit five: authenticity, heritage, community, pedagogy, criticism.
B
How does the authority-work overlay — recognition, exclusion, right-to-define, delegitimation — map onto those modes?
C
Do the Spanish-language and Anglophone publications differ in which modes they emphasize, and in where within the periodical those modes are performed?
†
Scope The study describes how legitimacy is constructed in print; it does not adjudicate which constructions are correct.

The five modes are not invented here.

Each mode is anchored in a body of scholarship that frames the concept and supplies its diagnostic features. LEGIT draws across all five in the Weberian / Bourdieusian sense, concretized for this corpus by four diagnostic acts.

Paratext layer A separate detector links each article to its recurring section — and the two periodicals are built differently. Candil sorts ≈1,518 articles into ~30 genre-differentiated editorial rubrics (Editorial, Ensayo, Discografía, Ponencia/comunicación, Coplas/poemas, Homenaje…), most persisting across the full 1979–1998 run. Jaleo's sections are fewer and functional/community (Juergas, Letters, Announcements, Reviews, San Diego Scene), and 59% of its 1,410 articles fall outside any recurring section. Detection is heuristic, but the structural contrast holds corpus-wide.

A hybrid pipeline, audited end-to-end.

Validity is part of the method, not a footnote: every quantitative claim in §6 is bounded by the audit's agreement statistics in Figure 3.

01
OCR & cleanup
scan → text, per-page confidence retained.
02
Candidate retrieval
regex + bilingual keyword index proposes passages to code.
03
LLM annotation
gpt-4o-mini · codebook + evidence window in; code + span out.
04
Article-level aggregation
passage codes rolled up to article-level family weights.
05 · gold
Human audit
stratified blind double-coding · reconciliation · κ on family & code.
†
Constraint The LLM is given a codebook entry and a small evidence window — never an article in full, never the periodical-level identity. This is the constraint that makes annotation auditable; it is also a limit (§8 · L4).

§5Methods — codebook & audit
06 / 15

Codebook v12.

Entman-style frames as competing modes of legitimacy. Codes are issued in FAMILY_NN form (e.g. AUTH_03, HERIT_11).

Each entry carries eight fields. The do-not-use-when clauses do the heaviest work — they name the collapse-risks between adjacent families.

The audit.

180 articles — 90 from Candil, 90 from Jaleo — drawn issue-stratified across the full span and coded by hand against the same codebook the LLM saw; 163 had usable LLM output to compare against.

Reconciled to gold labels. Human ↔ LLM κ at family and code level is reported in §6.3 — and it is the reliability bound that decides how far every automated claim can travel.

Audit · stratification
90 Candil + 73 Jaleo compared · issue-stratified · reconciled gold · 7,087 articles in the corpus ⇒ 2.3% coded by hand.

§7Interpretation
12 / 15

The interesting thing is not which mode wins — but where each is performed.

Fig 1 · description

Both periodicals lead with criticism; Candil leans more on authenticity, Jaleo far more on community. A description, not yet a claim.

Fig 2 · the claim

A periodical's recurring architecture is scaffolding for legitimacy work — but the two sort it differently: Candil distributes it across many sections, Jaleo concentrates it in Reviews.

Fig 3 · the bound

Family-level claims are defensible. Code-level claims — which sub-variety of authenticity, which register of community — are not, yet.

Structure is the finding. Totals are the description. The validity bound says how far the claim can travel.

§8Conclusion — limits
13 / 15

What the findings cannot say.

L1
Code-level LLM reliability is low.
Retrieval over-triggers a small set of broad codes and under-retrieves AUTH; family-level agreement is also chance-inflated by the unequal prior. All FAMILY_NN-level automated claims must be reread under this bound.
L2
The cross-context comparison is uneven.
Both periodicals are now audited (90 + 73), but Jaleo's recurring-section architecture is only sparsely detected — most audited Jaleo articles fall outside any named section — so the Spanish/Anglophone where-comparison is thinner on the Jaleo side.
L3
Section detection is heuristic.
The paratextual-scaffolding result in Fig 2 inherits whatever bias the recurring-section detector carries; the detector has not yet been validated on its own gold set.
L4
Trigger windows narrow the evidence the model sees.
The LLM is shown a passage around a keyword hit, not the article. The constraint that makes annotation auditable also forecloses article-level judgement.

§8Conclusion — directions
14 / 15

What comes next.

D1
Map legitimacy modes onto the section architecture — corpus-wide.
The section architecture is already established for the full corpus. What the rerun unlocks is lifting the section→mode pairing from the audited subset to the whole run. Jaleo's sparser, functional sectioning is a genuine feature of the periodical — not a detection gap to be closed — so the task is validating the mapping, not forcing Jaleo to mirror Candil.
D2
Move from trigger windows to article-level judgement.
A two-stage protocol: the LLM first reads the article, then is asked the codebook questions. The existing trigger-window output is retained as a check.
D3
Improve candidate retrieval.
The current regex/keyword index is the upstream cause of AUTH under-retrieval. A re-tuned bilingual index, jointly trained with the codebook's do-not-use-when clauses, is the most leveraged next change.
D4
Extend to peer periodicals.
El Olivo, Flamenco (LSE), La Caña — to test whether the paratextual-scaffolding finding generalizes beyond Candil's house style.
